# Shipped surface — what the built product does, and what it deliberately does not

**Owns:** a reader's overview of what `rawpage.exe` lets a person do today, stage by stage (§1), and
what is absent **by decision**, so that it is not reported as a defect (§2).

**Does not own:** per-task status, test figures and deviations (`Implementation-Plan.md` §4); what a
file does (`Architecture-FileMap.md` §3); the capability catalog and the key bindings (`Features.md`,
§8 for chords); go/no-go verdicts and their figures (`Tech-Stack-Plan.md` §3.3); issues (each stage's
testing guide, §8). Where those documents and this one disagree, they win and this one is corrected.

**Where it came from:** moved out of `CLAUDE.md` on 2026-10-16, where it had grown to some 60 KB of a
file loaded into every session. The text is that section as it stood after Stage 6 T14, unchanged.

**How it is kept:** when a task ships something a person can do, add it to §1 in the same change as
the task's `Implementation-Plan.md` row; when an absence is decided or filled, correct §2 **in place**.
A count quoted here is a figure from the dated run it names — re-derive it (`rawpage.exe
--list-actions`, the startup log's interface census) rather than quote it.

## 1. What works today

**What works today.** `rawpage.exe` opens real PDFs into tabs and draws their pages: a ribbon
(9 tabs, **49 groups**, 214 controls -- re-derived 2026-10-28 at Stage 6's sign-off from `res/ribbon.json`, and the product's startup log agrees (211 at Stage 6 T10, 2026-10-09; 200 at S4 T11, 2026-09-21; Stage 6's tools added the rest), and the group count is a
CORRECTION: 58 was the number of `"id":` lines in `res/ribbon.json`, which is 49 groups plus 9 tabs), a customizable Quick Access Toolbar, a tool finder, document tabs, a
keyboard, a Preferences dialog, a log pane and a live theme. A person can scroll, page through with
the keyboard, zoom by chord or `Ctrl`+wheel, fit four ways, rotate the view, switch the four layout
modes, type a page number, pan, marquee-zoom, and read a banner saying a file was repaired. The whole
**read** path is connected — `pdf/cos/` (lexer, objects, nine filters, xref, repair), `pdf/render/`
(the PDFium facade), `core/render/` (every decision about a tile except its pixels), `ui/viewer/`
(tiles onto a widget). **The save path exists below the UI** since Stage 3 T8 — `CommandJournal`,
`SaveCoordinator`, `pdf/ops/RevisionWriter` and `platform::IDurableFiles` save a rotation over the
whole editable corpus in tests — and **since T10 a person reaches it**: `Ctrl+R` rotates pages, `Ctrl+Z`/`Ctrl+Y` undo and redo,
`Ctrl+S`/`Ctrl+Shift+S` save, and closing a changed document asks first. **Since T11 a launch after a crash offers what was not saved**: recover it — into the file, or into an untitled document when the file changed — discard it, or decide later.
**And since Stage 4 T5 and T6 the sidebar holds three real panes.** Page thumbnails — one per page with its label, navigated by a click or `Enter`, walked with `↑`/`↓`, resized with `+`/`-`, rotated with `Ctrl+R` through Stage 3's command — rendered on the same scheduler and cache as the page, at `Speculative`, bounded to what the pane can show. Bookmarks, as the document's own tree with its own open/closed state, navigated by `Enter` and expanded with `→`/`←`/`*`/`/`. And named destinations, sortable by name or page, with one that goes nowhere shown as such rather than dropped. **All three navigate, and since T12 they navigate to the destination's VIEW and not only to its page**: `/XYZ`'s point and zoom, `/FitH`'s top, `/FitV`'s left, `/Fit`'s whole page and `/FitB`'s content box are applied, with a null in any of them meaning *keep what you have* as ISO 32000-1 §12.3.2.2 says — `/FitR`'s rectangle is the one thing still unread, because `core::Destination` has no fields for it (Stage 7's). That closed **I-05**, and applying it exposed a second defect worth knowing: **a fit re-satisfied against the page the viewport happens to be dominated by feeds back into the layout that decides which page that is**, so a bookmark to page 2 landed on page 4 until Fit Visible was anchored to the page it was set for. **And since T8 the page has something to measure it with**: two rulers in `units/rulerUnit`, reading the page from its drawn corner and tracking the cursor, whose corner box steps that unit to the next on a press (since 2026-10-03, `view.cycleRulerUnit`); a grid whose spacing, subdivisions, colour and origin are preferences; guides dragged off either ruler, moved, locked, deleted and cleared through commands; snapping to guides and the grid at **one threshold in DEVICE pixels**, so the tolerance is the same at 50 % and at 400 %; and `Ctrl+U`, `Ctrl+'` and `Ctrl+;` toggling the three of those that have a chord. **None of it is rasterized into a tile** — the grid, the guides and the page inversion are painted over the pixels the scheduler already returned, so changing the grid spacing re-renders nothing (D4-11). **And since T10 a document can be PRESENTED**: `F11` takes the window full screen and a *Presentation* command opens a surface of its own on a chosen monitor — one controller with two levels (D4-12), so both ask Windows to hold the display awake and every way out pairs that request. Pages turn with a transition the DOCUMENT chose where it has one and the preference chose where it does not, auto-advance steps at `/Dur` or at `fullScreen/autoAdvanceSec` and loops or stops, `B` and `W` blank the screen, `P` is a laser pointer, `Ctrl+L` hides the cursor and `Ctrl+Shift+M` moves it to the next display — and a monitor that disappears mid-slide moves the presentation rather than losing it. **And since T9 one document can be shown in up to four panes of one window**: split it with a horizontal divider, a vertical one, or a spreadsheet freeze that pins a top row and a left column while the body scrolls — over **one handle, one journal and one revision** (D4-13), so a `Ctrl+R` in either half rotates the page in both; the panes scroll together by default and `window.syncScroll` frees them, and *Remove Split* puts the window back to one view. **And since T11 there can be more than one window and the product arranges them**: tile horizontally or vertically, cascade, *Merge All Windows*, and a window list — all over this process's own windows with Qt geometry and no new `platform/` interface (D4-17), per monitor, and refused rather than inert with one window open. **`window.new` stopped meaning *an empty window* and became a second VIEW of the document in front**: its own `DocumentHandle`, because two windows each run the three-step close protocol, and **no second journal**, because two journals over one file would be two Saves racing for the same bytes — so it lays out from the first window's model tree at its revision and an unsaved rotation shows in both, and when the first closes the second detaches and goes on drawing the file. The two scroll together by default; `window.syncScroll` frees them, and it is one switch with two subjects (a split where there is one, the windows where there is not). **And the window that is not in front now queues its viewport at `Prefetch`** — A2's named work, built — so a second window can no longer take the single render worker away from the one a person is looking at. **And since T13 the product remembers the session**: a clean quit writes what was open — the documents and their tab order, the tab in front, each one's layout mode, zoom intent, page, scroll and guides, each window's split and panes, and the geometry keyed by the monitor arrangement it was on — and the next launch puts it back **third**, after the crash-recovery offer and after the command line's files, skipping any path those already opened so one file is never in two tabs. It never brings back an EDIT: a document closed with *Don't Save* comes back at the bytes on disk, because unsaved work belongs to the recovery journal and two mechanisms restoring one edit would restore it twice. `general/restoreSession` off writes nothing and restores nothing; `general/rememberWindowLayout` and `general/rememberPaneLayout` subtract the geometry and the panes. **And since T12 `Ctrl+3` finally means what `Features.md` §1.2 says it means**: Fit Visible fits — and scrolls to — the page's **content bounding box**, the union of its object bounds taken through the engine and cached per page, falling back to the CropBox on a page with no objects. That closes **Stage 2's I-28**, which had made `Ctrl+3` a second name for `Ctrl+0` on every document whose CropBox is its sheet. Beside it: `Ctrl+D` opens **Document Properties** — the Info dictionary, the version, the page count, the current page's size, the security and repair lines, the path and the size — which **edits nothing and constructs no command**; `Ctrl+L` is **reading mode**, the chrome gone and the window kept, restoring the panes, widths and dock sides it hid rather than a default layout; and the page box speaks **logical page labels**, so a reader on the page printed "1" sees "1" with the physical index beside it and can type `iv` or `A-12` where the document has one. **And since T7 the page surface has real tools**: Hand, Select, Marquee Zoom and a Loupe, armed stickily, with `Shift` held giving a temporary hand without disarming anything, `Esc` abandoning a drag and leaving the view exactly where it was, and auto-scroll at a signed speed that stops at the end of the document. **And since Stage 5 T5 a person can select and copy TEXT**: the engine thread extracts a page's characters with their boxes (`core/text/PageTextModel`, T2), `LayoutAnalyzer` orders them into lines, blocks, columns and tables by geometry (T4), and the Selection tool drags in that reading order — across pages and from one column to the next — selects a word, a line or a block on two, three or four clicks, a column on `Alt`+drag, and extends on `Shift`+click; `Ctrl+C` writes plain text, HTML and, for a column, a table in one copy, and `Ctrl+A` selects the whole document, its unread pages extracted in the background when it is copied. Glyphs whose font gives no Unicode are refused, not pasted. **And since T6 there is SEARCH below the UI**: `core/text/DocumentIndex` keeps every extracted page per document identity -- shared by a `window.new` companion, its own budget `search/indexCacheMB`, a rotation's `Scope` dropping the page -- and `core/text/SearchEngine` matches a query over it on workers of its own, every flag (whole words, case, diacritics, regex) through T4's one normalizer, never across a paragraph, with the bound said and unmapped glyphs reported; the selection reads its pages from the same index. The ten `search/*` keys have their Preferences page (nine of 26). **And since T7 a person can FIND**: `Ctrl+F` opens a find bar above the page -- one per tab -- that searches as you type (a 200 ms pause, the newer search cancelling the older), highlights every hit over the page and the current one deeper, steps with `F3`/`Shift+F3` and `Ctrl+G`/`Ctrl+Shift+G` and wraps saying so, takes the view to the current hit, toggles regex, whole words and case with `Alt+R`/`Alt+W`/`Alt+C` -- which ARE the settings -- searches the whole document, the selection or a page range, reports an invalid regular expression rather than *no matches*, says how many glyphs had no Unicode, and `Esc` closes it and clears every highlight. **Since T14 it never widens the window**: where the row is too narrow, the toggles, *Highlight all* and the scope fold behind a *More* button into a popup (S5 guide I-10 -- until then `Ctrl+F` widened a 900-pixel window to 1,102). **And since T8 the find bar searches beyond the page text**: comments, form fields, bookmarks and attachments, each only when its `search/include*` switch on Preferences' Search page is on -- read through ONE facade reader, `IDocumentBackend::searchUnits`, a part at a time (a page's annotations, the outline, the attachments) as an engine task on the render thread, once per document; a comment or field hit goes to its rectangle, a bookmark hit to its destination, and an attachment hit -- its name, its description, or the text of an attached PDF one level deep -- is selected and opens nothing, the status line naming the class. An attachment's `/Desc` is read first-party (`pdf/ops/EmbeddedFileReader`), because PDFium's attachment API cannot. **And since T9 there is ADVANCED SEARCH**: `Ctrl+Shift+F` opens a dock that searches a FOLDER, recursive or not, with every flag and content class -- each file opened in pieces, its engine work queued behind every visible tile and the rest on a helper thread, so the UI thread opens nothing -- never a tab and never a password (an encrypted file is listed as skipped); results stream in per file with snippets, sort by relevance (the file's hit count), page or file, filter by author, subject and date, refine, and export as CSV through the stage's one planned waiver; opening a result shows its hits in that file's tab. **And since T10 there is a SNAPSHOT**: `S` arms a tool whose dragged band is re-rendered -- not grabbed -- through the same scheduler, bypassing the tile cache, at `pageDisplay/snapshotDpi` (0 as displayed, 72 to 600 dpi, bounded at 50 megapixels and said when reduced), from the one page the band is mostly over, onto white, and put on the clipboard as an image with the status bar saying its size and resolution; none of the inversion, grid, guides, selection or highlights is in it, and `Esc` or a click copies nothing. **And since T11 there is REFLOW VIEW**: `Ctrl+4` replaces the page surface with a read-only text surface of the analyzer's blocks in reading order, re-wrapped to the window in Qt's fonts at the model's size, weight and italic -- a window of pages round the reader's place, grown as they scroll -- entering at the block at the top of the page and returning with the reflow's top block at the top of the page; the zoom chords set its text size, it claims the reading keys, it asks for no raster, and a find hit takes the tab back to the page. **And since T12 there is a COMPARER below the UI**: `core/text/DocumentComparer` aligns two documents' pages, diffs each pair's words (Myers, linear space, under a cap that reports unrelated pages *replaced*), finds moves, and at its *appearance* sensitivity compares 72 dpi rasters outside what the words claimed -- with the header/footer band, page numbers and whitespace each ignorable -- on a thread of its own (`ComparisonRun`). **And since T13 a person can COMPARE**: `Ctrl+Alt+C` opens a dialog for the older and the newer file -- open tabs or any path -- the sensitivity and the three ignore-lists, and a window of its own shows the two side by side, each over a handle of the comparison's own and neither through a journal, scrolled together by ALIGNED page so the page after an insertion sits beside its counterpart, or as an overlay in which ink both share is grey, ink only the older has red and ink only the newer has blue; the Differences pane lists every change in page order with a count by kind, and `Ctrl+]`/`Ctrl+[` step both views and the pane, wrapping with a message. Its bar's sensitivity and ignore switches ARE the four `documents/compare*` settings, and changing one re-runs the comparison over the text already read; every other key pressed in it is claimed, so nothing reaches the tab behind it. Its text is read through the index at `Background`, analyzed and diffed off the UI thread, and its rasters bypass the tile cache. **And since the walk's own changes (2026-10-03 and 2026-10-04, S5 guide A-115 … A-129)**: the rulers' corner box steps the unit; Advanced Search's `Enter` narrows a list that exists, *Clear* empties it, and a refined result opens under the query it was refined by; the sidebar, the log pane and the search pane draw the edge that faces the page; the Compare dialog's newer field offers the file the older one was last compared against (`core/settings/ComparePairs`, the internal key `documents/recentComparisons`); the comparison's overlay has a fit and a zoom of its own, scrolls, turns to the next pair at its edge and rasterizes only the region in view, and its *Continuous* switch lays every pair one under another in one scroll; and `Ctrl`+wheel steps Reflow view's text size as the zoom chords do.

**And Stage 6, signed off 2026-10-28 (T1 to T15; T15, 2026-10-20, re-measured and swept and shipped nothing a person does)**: the annotation corpus; every annotation read first-party (`pdf/ops/AnnotationReader` into `core/doc/AnnotationStore`) and held to agree with PDFium page by page, a disagreeing page read-only; `AddAnnotationCommand` and `DeleteAnnotationCommand` writing Highlight end to end with our own `/AP`, saved incrementally and verified; **what the screen shows is what Save writes** -- after every command the journal's show step composes and verifies the update and the engine reloads it (D6-2), an annotation edit keeps the page's text and redraws only the tiles its rectangle reaches. **And since T5 a person can handle the annotations already on a page**: click one -- hit where it is DRAWN, a rectangle by its outline and a line by its stroke (D6-30) -- drag it, resize it by a handle (`Shift` keeping proportions), `Alt`+drag a copy, nudge it with the arrows (one undo step per burst), `Delete` it, recolour, re-word or lock it from the properties bar (`Ctrl+E`), and read a note's pop-up on hover -- and, since 2026-10-20, a caret's, holding the words inserted (S6 guide I-26); a `Locked` one is selected and not moved (D6-27); every gesture is ONE command (`annot.move`, `annot.edit`, `annot.setAppearance`, `annot.lock`), a refusal said in the status bar, an undo after a save putting back the file's own dictionary. The fourteen `Ctrl+Alt+` chords ARM their tools and put the page in Annotation tools, which resolves through the Viewer. **And since T6 a person can MARK and NOTE**: the highlighter, underline, strikeout and squiggly drag across text as the Selection tool does -- across pages and columns, a word on two clicks -- and mark exactly the runs selected (graded against the glyphs' ink on two columns, a page at `/Rotate` 90 and vertical CJK); a command pressed with text already selected marks it at once; *Replace Text* strikes the words out under a caret holding the replacement, *Insert Text* puts a caret between two letters, and the sticky note (`Ctrl+Alt+M`) puts Acrobat's `Comment` note and its pop-up where the click was -- the text TYPED FIRST into a small editor, so each is one command and one `Ctrl+Z`; every one is by `identity/author`, or the Windows sign-in name `app/` supplies when that is empty (the Identity page, ten of 26). A tool that made something goes back to the Selection tool unless `K` keeps it. **And since T7 a person can TYPE a comment onto the page**: a text box dragged out (or clicked), a callout pressed on what it points at and dragged to its box, and typewriter text clicked anywhere (`annot.typewriter`, chordless) -- one `FreeText` each, the text typed FIRST into an editor over the box at its own size, made ONCE and fitted to what was typed (`core/doc/TypedText`), a double click editing it in place as one more command. Latin is drawn in Helvetica and Chinese, Japanese and Korean in the four predefined CJK fonts, none embedded -- which collection draws an ideograph is the person's input language unless kana or Hangul decides it -- and **a character none of them can show (Greek, Hebrew, Cyrillic …) is refused AS IT IS TYPED**, the status bar naming it (`DW-3`); which CJK characters are shown is a generated table held to PDFium character by character. **And since T8 a person can DRAW**: a line, an arrow, a rectangle and an oval dragged out (`Shift` keeps 45 degrees and squares), a polygon, a polyline and a cloud clicked corner by corner and finished with `Enter` or a double click, ink drawn with the pencil -- one stroke one `Ink`, its curve through every sample -- and erased with the eraser, one undo step a gesture; each one command, drawn by our own builder with its dashes, line endings and blend mode, the very bytes Save writes. Each tool's colour, width, style, opacity, blend, fill, font size and endings start from `commenting/default<Property>.<tool>` -- set on the Commenting page (eleven of 26) or written from the properties bar by `Ctrl+Alt+Enter` -- and `K` keeps a tool armed. **And since T9 a person can MANAGE the comments**: `Ctrl+Shift+C` opens the Comments pane -- every comment of the document, grouped by page, author, day, type, status or checkmark, sorted, filtered and searched (a thread found by any reply's text, a hidden comment listed and said to be) -- and the page follows the list; `Ctrl+Enter` replies (a note under the comment, by the person) -- and on the page a note's pop-up is its THREAD's, the note and every reply with its author, reached from the note or any reply over it, scrolling when long, with a **Reply** button at its foot whose editor makes the reply (S6 guide I-29) -- `Space` is their own checkmark, `Ctrl+Shift+K` sets a review status whose history the list shows, `F2` edits, `Delete` takes a comment with its thread, and *Delete all* and *Delete matching* are one undo step each; `Ctrl+.`/`Ctrl+,` step through the comments from the page too, and numbering paints sequence numbers over the page without writing them. **Since 2026-10-22** (S6 guide I-32 … I-34) a right click on a comment offers Reply, Edit in Place, Open Editor, its status and Delete; every delete of comments -- the pane's, the bulk ones and the page's -- asks first; each row starts with a picture of its kind; and ONE CLICK -- on a comment on the page, or on its row -- opens its pop-up, for every kind holding text but typed text, until the person presses anywhere else. Each reply and status is one command, and a status is a hidden state reply as Acrobat writes it. The find bar searches an open document's comments from its model, so one not saved yet is found -- and selected. **And since T10 a person can STAMP**: `Ctrl+Alt+S` arms the stamp tool and shows the palette -- Table 181's fourteen standard stamps, five dynamic ones that bake who placed them and when, and the person's own -- and a click places the chosen stamp at its natural size, a drag fits it to a box; the properties bar turns one to any angle and a handle resizes it, each one command; a custom stamp is made from an image (a PNG with its alpha, a JPEG kept byte for byte) or from initials drawn or typed, filed in a library that survives a restart and a damaged file -- moved to another category chosen from a list or named anew -- never as a built-in heading (S6 guide I-42) -- several made at once from several images, each named in a list (I-43), and any stamp added to Favourites, the palette's first heading, by a right click on it; the palette's highlight goes when the tool is put away, so one click places the same stamp again (S6 guide I-39 … I-41); an image on the clipboard pastes as a stamp. **And since T11 a person can ATTACH**: `Ctrl+Shift+A` opens the Attachments pane -- the document's files and every pushpin's, with description, size, date and page -- whose buttons open, save, add and delete, each a command; an attached PDF opens in a tab of its own from its bytes, read-only until saved as a file; a program or a script is refused; any other file is asked about first, then written to this session's folder (emptied at a clean quit) and opened with the program Windows uses for it; `annot.attachFile` pins a chosen file where the page is clicked; a find hit on an attachment selects it in the pane; a large payload goes to a side file of the recovery journal. **And since T12 a person can MEASURE**: `Ctrl+Alt+D` measures a distance (a drag, or a click at each end), `annot.measurePerimeter` and `Ctrl+Alt+A` a perimeter and an area (a click at each point, `Enter`, a double click or a right click -- the last since Stage 6 guide I-48), the pointer hidden while a snap's marker shows (I-49), and `annot.dimension` draws a dimension callout standing off the points on leader lines -- each point snapping to the drawing's own corners, midpoints and crossings (and, with `measuring/snapToPaths`, to its paths) within the one device-pixel threshold, `Shift` holding 45 degrees, the reading shown as the pointer moves; each is one annotation carrying its scale in its own `/Measure`, its reading drawn on the page and written to its comment, and listed in the properties bar's history. The scale is set from two lengths (`annot.measurementScale`) or calibrated from one measured (`annot.measureCalibrate`), and the Measuring page holds its keys. **And since T13 a person can EXCHANGE**: *Import* reads an FDF or an XFDF -- Acrobat's or ours -- and merges it by `/NM` as ONE command, a comment newer by `/M` replacing the document's, an older one kept, and each one that cannot come (a locked one, a page the document lacks, a kind this build cannot write yet) refused by name in a report while the rest come; *Export* writes what the Comments pane lists, with each one's thread, as either; an XFDF holding a document type declaration is refused before any entity exists; *Migrate Comments* carries an older revision's comments to the tab in front -- markup by the words it covers, any other comment beside its nearest line of prose, over Stage 5's page alignment -- and reports, never places, what it cannot find; and `Ctrl+C`, `Ctrl+X` and `Ctrl+V` carry annotations between tabs as RawPage's own clipboard type, each paste a new `/NM`. **And since T14 a person can MARK FOR REDACTION and RECORD**: `Ctrl+Alt+K` arms the redaction tool -- a drag across text marks its words, a line a region, and a drag anywhere else marks an area; text already selected is marked at once -- each mark outlined in red and carrying the fill and the overlay text (once or repeated, set on the properties bar) it will leave when applied, and REMOVING NOTHING: every content stream of the page is the same bytes after it, and applying is Stage 10's. `annot.recordAudio` records a sound comment where the page is clicked, `annot.attachAudio` takes one from a PCM WAV (anything else refused by its format), and a double click plays one -- through Qt Multimedia's Media Foundation backend, never FFmpeg's; the samples are saved exactly as recorded and come back out the same.

**And Stage 7, in progress: since T6 (2026-11-04) Save As REWRITES.** `Ctrl+Shift+S` writes the document as one revision of what it still reaches -- what a page delete or a replace left behind is gone, so deleting 800 of a 900-page file's pages and Saving As makes it smaller, where Save makes it larger -- with every object at its own number, so undo, redo and the annotations' identities carry on across it, and an undo of a delete after it brings the deleted page back. *Save a Copy* (`file.saveACopy`) writes the same file elsewhere and leaves the tab on its own file with its changes unsaved. `Ctrl+S` still appends, unless Preferences' Documents page sets *How Save writes the file* (`documents/saveMode`) to *Rewrite the whole file*. A document carrying a signature is ASKED about first -- *Keep the signatures* appends, as Save does, and the signatures still verify; *Rewrite* names them as becoming invalid -- and with nobody to ask (a headless run) the signatures are kept. An encrypted document, which this build reads and does not edit, is Saved As as a copy of its bytes. **Since T7 (2026-11-05) pages make new documents.** *Extract Pages* (`page.extract`, the ribbon's Organize tab; `Ctrl+Shift+X` is the Page Thumbnails pane's chord, whose selection T8 fills in) takes a range typed as `1-3, 5, 8-` and makes either ONE new document -- opened as an *Untitled* tab, read-only and unsaved until it is Saved As, closing it asks first, and `Ctrl+S` asks where -- or one file per page in a chosen folder; *delete the extracted pages* removes them from the document in one undo step, and only once the new document is made and checked. *Split Document* (`page.split`, the same tab) writes a folder of files every N pages, at every top-level bookmark or down to a chosen level, by a maximum file size, at blank pages (left out of the files) or at pages whose text matches a pattern (literal or a regular expression), each named after the document and numbered. Both take the document AS IT STANDS, unsaved edits included, write nothing of it, and never write over a file: a name the folder already holds refuses the whole split before anything is written. **Since T8 (2026-11-07) the Page Thumbnails pane EDITS** (`Stage7-Assembly-Plan.md` D7-13, D7-31). It selects a SET as a file manager does -- a click selects one page and goes to it, `Ctrl`+click adds or removes one, `Shift`+click and `Shift`+arrow take the run from where the last click was, `Ctrl+A` takes every page -- and a set stays put while the page view scrolls. Every row of its `Features.md` §8 table works on the set, each ONE undo step: `Delete` removes it with no question, `Alt+↑`/`Alt+↓` move it one place earlier or later (a scattered set is gathered as it moves), `Ctrl+Shift+D` duplicates it, `Ctrl+R` opens Rotate Pages offering the set, `Ctrl+Shift+X` opens Extract with the set filled in, and `Ctrl+X`/`Ctrl+C`/`Ctrl+V` cut, copy and paste PAGES -- a paste lands after the set, and pages copied in one RawPage window or process paste in another, with no file between them; a text field keeps its own `Ctrl+X`/`C`/`V` while it has the focus. A DRAG of thumbnails moves the set to where the bar across the column shows, and dropped on ANOTHER window's pane it copies the pages there, the document they came from unchanged. Each thumbnail carries small marks for comments, form fields and links, named in a tooltip. The ribbon's Organize tab now runs *Insert Pages* -- from a PDF (a range of it, or every page), blank pages of a chosen size (Preferences' Documents page sets the size it starts at, *Size of a new blank page*, A4 unless changed), or the pages on the clipboard -- *Delete Pages* (a range), *Replace Pages* (with pages from a PDF, keeping the pages' places so what pointed at them still lands on them), and Rotate Pages takes a range, odd or even pages, and portrait or landscape pages only. After a page command the status bar says what it did to what pointed at the pages -- *Bookmarks: 1 lost their destination; Named destinations: 1 removed* -- and before the first change to a SIGNED document the message bar says that signature checkers will report it as changed, once, and the change is made.
