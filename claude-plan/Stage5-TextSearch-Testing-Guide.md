## 1. Scope

### In scope — must pass for sign-off

- **Text is extracted truthfully**, with geometry, from every corpus file that has text, and **text
  that cannot be extracted is reported as such** rather than extracted as plausible garbage.
- **Selection is accurate on multi-column, RTL, vertical and CJK pages**, across page boundaries,
  and **matches the glyphs a person sees**. Copy delivers plain text, HTML and a table.
- **Find and Advanced Search honour every §1.8 toggle across all five content classes**, over one
  document, a selection, a page range and a folder; **the second search of a 900-page file is
  instant** by §3's definition.
- **Snapshot** copies a region as the document draws it. **Reflow view** re-wraps text in reading
  order.
- **Comparison finds text, appearance and page-level changes**, honours its ignore-lists and
  sensitivities, and stays bounded on documents that have nothing in common.
- **Nothing in this stage changes a document.** `mutation-lint` reports **one** command on the last
  day of the stage as it did on the first (D5-1).
- **The A2-at-Stage-5 verdict, the layout baseline and O7's revisit are recorded.**
- **No regression** in any Stage 0 to 4 case, on **both** presets.

### What this evidence cannot tell us

- **Every automated claim is about our own generated corpus.** Its fonts are rectangles, its layouts
  are regular and its producer is us (D2-11). A green `tst_layoutcorpus` says *"geometry recovers the
  structure of documents we generated"* — **never** *"reading order is correct"*, and nothing about
  a real document. **And no manual case supplies one at Stage 5** (plan D5-23): the manual half walks
  generated documents too, so the bound `golden/README.md` puts on the golden suite applies to
  **every** Stage 5 result, and the analyzer's accuracy on real layouts is carried to R1's row
  unmeasured.
- **`ui/` is outside every sanitizer this project has** (O10, `Implementation-Plan.md` §8).
  `portable-asan` and `fuzz_text_pipeline` cover `core/text/` — the normalizer, the analyzer,
  selection, search, the index and the comparer — and nothing about the find bar, the panes or the
  views.
- **An offscreen widget run has no clipboard a real application pastes from.** CF_HTML is read back
  through Win32 on the `windows` platform (A-50), but whether Word renders it well is M-02's.
- **Latency is measured; responsiveness is judged.** T3 measures the viewport's p95 queued latency,
  the quantity A2 is graded on. Whether scrolling *feels* smooth while a first search indexes 900
  pages is M-06's, and the two can disagree.

### Case anatomy

Every case is **ID · Goal · Steps · Expect · If it fails**, with a `☐` changed to ✅ or ❌. A case
either passes or yields a **specific** failure — "looks fine" is not a result.

### The rules this guide is written under

**Rule 1 — assert the EFFECT, not the intent** (Stage 2's I-28, convention 19). A selection range
is not a highlight, a hit list is not a highlighted glyph, a `Background` flag is not a viewport that
was not starved. Where a case can only reach the intent, it says so at the assertion and names what
it therefore does not cover.

**Rule 2 — a repaired case is re-walked from step 1** (Stage 1's I-45).

**Rule 3 — a green tick is not a clean run** (Stage 2's I-38). Linux-only halves are read from the
CI job's **log**, never from its tick.

**Rule 4 — a widget case drives layout itself, and asserts a non-zero visible count first**
(convention 12).

**Rule 5 — a new mechanism gets a control written from what must NOT happen** (convention 22). A
toggle that is off must not match; a hyphen mid-line must not be joined; an identical pair must
report nothing; a text field must keep its own `Ctrl+C`.

**Rule 6 — the oracle for GEOMETRY is the rendered ink** (Stage 4's I-10). A highlight, a hit
rectangle or a snapshot is compared against the **raster** of the page, located by code that shares
nothing with `PageTextModel`'s transform. A case that checks a highlight against the char box it was
computed from proves the arithmetic agrees with itself.

**Rule 7 — the oracle for TEXT is the generator**, never PDFium. Each fixture's expected text,
order and structure is what `corpus/make-text.py` and `corpus/make-compare.py` wrote down when they
built it (plan §5.2). A case that compares the extractor with `FPDFText_GetText` proves PDFium agrees
with itself.

**Rule 8 — both presets, and the UI binary whole.** `x64-debug` fail-fasts where `x64-release`
reports green (Stage 4's I-09), and a UI case that leaves process state behind breaks a case that
runs after it in the same process (Stage 4's I-28). Every run of the suite is both presets, and T14
runs `rawpage_ui_tests.exe` whole under a printed seed.

### Severity

| Sev | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **S1** | **A Stage 5 surface changes the document** — any byte on disk, any journal entry, any `Commands.def` row. **The product presents text that is not in the document** — unmapped glyphs shown as extracted text, a copy that invents characters. **The window stops rendering** while text work runs, or a folder search freezes it. A crash on a corpus file |
| **S2** | A documented behaviour is wrong in a way a later stage builds on — a reading order, a selection that does not match the glyphs, a toggle not honoured, a hit missed or invented, a change the comparison misses, a layout target missed |
| **S3** | A message is unclear, a snippet is badly cut, a figure is misreported, a diagnostic names the wrong thing |
| **S4** | Polish nobody outside the team would notice |

**The S1 that is new at this stage is *text that is not there*.** A wrong pixel is visible and a
wrong character is not: a clipboard, an exported list or a comparison carries it into someone else's
work, where nobody knows to check it.

## 2. Build, launch, environment

`cmake`, `ninja` and `g++` are **not on this machine's `PATH`**. Every command assumes
`scripts\env.ps1` has been dot-sourced first.

```powershell
. .\scripts\env.ps1
cmake --preset x64-release
cmake --build --preset x64-release --target checks     # four gates; mutation-lint must say ONE command
cmake --build --preset x64-release
ctest --preset x64-release --output-on-failure
ctest --preset x64-release -R "textmodel|normalize|layout|selection|search|index|compare"
ctest --preset x64-release -R "extract|layoutcorpus|comparecorpus"      # the engine-backed halves
.\build\x64-release\bin\rawpage_bench_pipeline.exe --stage5            # T3's A2 figure
.\build\x64-release\bin\rawpage.exe corpus\ordinary\columns-two.pdf
```

**Notes carried from `CLAUDE.md`** because they still cost time: PowerShell does not wait on a GUI
binary (`Start-Process -Wait -PassThru`); its pipeline captures none of `rawpage.exe`'s output (use
`cmd` redirection); a running `rawpage.exe` blocks the release link and skips a case; a failing
run's output survives only until the next run, in `build\<preset>\Testing\Temporary\LastTest.log`.

**Where things are written**, so a walker can look:

| What | Where |
|---|---|

| A manual walk's own files | `tests\manual\<case>`, and nowhere else (`CLAUDE.md`) — a **copy** of the corpus file, never the corpus file itself, which is generated and checked byte for byte |

### 3.2 The layout baseline (D5-7)

Measured by `tst_layoutcorpus` over every page with a label file, against the generator's labels.
**These targets are the pass bar for T4.** A miss is an S2 in §8: the analyzer is improved, or you
change the target with the measurement beside it — never quietly.

| Measure | Definition | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **Line grouping** | F1 over lines, a predicted line matching a labelled one when their character sets are equal | **≥ 0.98** |
| **Block grouping** | F1 over blocks, matched the same way | **≥ 0.95** |
| **Columns** | Pages whose detected column count equals the label | **≥ 95 %** |
| **Reading order** | Of each labelled pair of consecutive blocks, the fraction that appear in the same relative order in the predicted order | **≥ 0.95** |
| **Tables, detection** | Precision and recall over table regions, matched at intersection-over-union ≥ 0.8; the unruled aligned table counts, the tabular-looking prose is a negative | **≥ 0.90 each** |
| **Tables, grid** | Of correctly detected tables, the fraction whose rows × columns equal the label | **≥ 0.80** |

**Recorded as evidence for R1** (`Tech-Stack-Plan.md` §10), with the bound: *"on our own generated
corpus"*.

### 3.3 *"The second search of a 900-page file is instant"*

The phrase is `Implementation-Plan.md`'s. **Defined here as:** after the index of
`ordinary/text-900.pdf` is complete, a literal query's **every hit delivered within 100 ms at p95**
over 20 queries, measured from submission to the last hit on the UI thread, on this machine. 100 ms
is the usual threshold below which a response reads as immediate. The **first** search, which builds
the index, is reported and **not** graded — it is A2's subject (§3.1), not this one's.

**Graded 2026-10-01 at T6 (A-63): INSTANT — p95 13.23 ms** over twenty literal queries and 142,840 hits
(a second run 12.48 ms), the first search 1,708 ms, on this machine with the case driving the pump; the
planted control, every delivery 150 ms late, failed the grade at 172.8 ms. **The bound:** the product
delivers through Qt's event queue (`app/CompositionRoot`'s pump), which this figure does not include.

### 3.4 Comparison on generated pairs

Over every `corpus/compare/` pair, against its `expected.json`: **every labelled change found, with
its kind**; **zero** reported differences on the identical pair at every sensitivity; **no
difference outside the labelled ones** at *text only*. The unrelated pair finishes **within 10
seconds** with **peak live heap under 256 MB** (measured, as Stage 2's I-35 taught, as LIVE heap and
never as private bytes). The walkable pair, `report-v1` → `report-v2`, is graded here like every
pair and judged by a person in M-08 — **no real revision pair is compared at Stage 5** (D5-23).

**Result, 2026-10-02 (T12): met on every count, on our own generated pairs.** All twelve edit pairs and the
walkable one found every labelled change with its kind (A-95); at *text only* **no difference outside the
labelled ones** on any pair; the identical pair **0 differences at all three sensitivities and all eight
ignore-list combinations** (A-96); the unrelated pair **0.013 and 0.017 s on two runs, with a live-heap peak of 54.2 MB** (54.2 MB
before it began), every one of its 50 pairs *replaced* past the cap (A-97). Two bounds on the claim: the
pairs are ours, one kind of change each in base-14 Helvetica (`corpus/make-compare.py`), and the 10 s is the
comparison's, over text the engine had already extracted -- extraction is the engine thread's and is A2's
column (§3.1), not this one.

## 7. Known caveats — verify, do not assume broken

Behaviours that look like defects and are not. **A wrong row here is worse than a missing one.**

| Looks broken | Is |
|---|---|
| **Some documents' text cannot be selected, copied or found** | Correct when the font carries no usable Unicode mapping — the product reports it rather than inventing text (D5-2). Record which document; a document whose text **is** mappable and is not found is the defect |
| **Whole-words-only finds single CJK characters inside longer words** | Correct at Stage 5: without a dictionary segmenter each ideograph is a word (D5-5) |
| **`ß` is not found by `SS`** | Correct: Qt's case folding is simple folding (D5-5, A-33) |
| **The Hebrew in `rtl-text.pdf` is drawn as blocks, not letters** | Correct — the corpus embeds no third-party font, and nothing else reaches Hebrew (plan D5-23). The paste into Notepad is what reads |
| **A tagged document reads in geometric order** | Correct until Stage 7 reads its tags (D5-6) |
| **Reflow view loses the pictures and the typefaces** | Correct — it is text re-wrapped (D5-16) |
| **The first search of a long document takes a while; the second does not** | Correct — the first builds the index at background priority (D5-3, D5-4) |
| **A search hit in an attachment does not open it** | Correct until Stage 6's Attachments pane (D5-12) |
| **A pasted snapshot blurs when scaled up in the editor** | Correct at the default, *as displayed* — it is a bitmap (D5-10, `DW-2`). Raising `pageDisplay/snapshotDpi` is the answer (D5-11) |
| **The comparison view has no rulers** | Correct (D5-18) |
| **A folder search skips a protected file** | Correct — a folder search never prompts (D5-13) |
| **`Ctrl+C` in a tree pane copies the page selection** | Correct — `Ctrl+C` is §8's Common row, and a pane with nothing of its own to copy falls through to it (D5-9) |

**I-16 · The ribbon's key tips stayed on screen after the focus or the mouse went elsewhere.** *Mechanism:* `ui/shell/Ribbon` shows its `Alt` key tips (R-5, `Stage1-AppShell-Plan.md` §5.3) by taking the focus, so the next letter lands in its `keyPressEvent`, and removed them only on `Esc`, at the end of the sequence, or on `Alt` again. A click elsewhere, `Tab`, or another window took the focus and left the tips drawn -- and the letters they showed then went to the widget that had the focus: on the page, the Home tab's `H` is the page's own `H`. The next `Alt` HID the stale tips rather than showing them, and gave the focus back to wherever it was before they appeared, taking it from where the person had put it. A press on a widget that takes no focus left the ribbon holding a focus nothing could type into. *Found* by the user before M-03 (2026-10-02): *"the tips shown for Alt will not disappear after user changes the focus or using mouse to click on other things, which may lead to misoperation if there are some collision of same keybindings."* *Why the suite missed it:* R-5's criterion is *"dismissable with `Esc`"*, and every key-tip case -- Stage 1's `Alt key tips are reachable through the registry`, Stage 4's A-102 -- drove the tips with keys alone; none moved the focus or pressed the mouse while they were up (convention 22: the criterion asked only that they go when they should be asked to). *Fix:* the tips last while the ribbon has the focus. `Ribbon::focusOutEvent` dismisses them and leaves the focus where it went; an application event filter, installed only while they are up and watching mouse presses only -- the keys stay `ShortcutBinder`'s (D1-7) -- dismisses them on a press anywhere, giving the focus back first so a press on a widget that takes none leaves the person where they were, and the press goes on unchanged. The tips are deleted later rather than at once, because a press on a tip reaches the filter with that tip as its receiver. *Regression:* `ui.the key tips go when the focus leaves the ribbon or the mouse is pressed` (`[shell][I-16]`): the focus moved elsewhere -- gone, the focus kept there, and `Alt` then SHOWS them; a press on the status bar, which takes no focus -- gone, the focus back where it was before them; and its control, what belongs to the sequence keeps them -- a letter no tip shows, and the step from the tabs' tips to the groups'. **Planted controls, three, each fired:** no focus-out dismissal (the focus half), no mouse dismissal (the press half), and a press dismissing without giving the focus back (that check alone). *What it does not cover:* another WINDOW taking the focus, which the offscreen platform does not raise -- the same `focusOutEvent` answers it, with `Qt::ActiveWindowFocusReason`. **Checked by the user on the rebuilt binary the same day: *"It's fixed."***

**I-15 · The selection was barely visible on the page.** *Mechanism:* `PageView::selectionColour` painted the palette's `Highlight` at alpha 96 over the page, its comment saying the colour was judged in both themes (convention 20). In the light theme that token is `SelectionBackground`, `#cce0f5` -- a pale tint chosen for the chrome, where it is drawn OPAQUE behind list text -- so made translucent again over white paper it came out about `#ECF3FB`: **1.12:1 from the page beside it**, and the translucent fill greyed the black glyphs under it to **5.1:1**. The dark theme's token, `#2d537a`, came out visible, which is why the comment's claim held in one theme. *Found* by the user after M-02's walk (2026-10-02): *"it's not clear to see which part of text and the area being selected."* *Why the suite missed it:* A-42 … A-48 judge WHERE the highlight lies -- every selected glyph's ink inside it, every other glyph's outside -- and nothing measured whether it can be SEEN; the page is not chrome, so no theme check reached it. *Decision (the user's, 2026-10-02, of three -- a stronger translucent band, Word-style inverted glyphs, or a tinted paper):* **the paper is tinted and the glyphs keep their ink.** *Fix:* the colour is a constant of the page, not of the theme (`searchHitColour`'s argument), MULTIPLIED onto the page -- white paper becomes `#99C4F0` and black ink stays black -- and on an inverted page, whose paper is black and which a multiply leaves black, SCREENED on as `#1A447A`, so the paper becomes a dark blue and the white glyphs stay white. `SelectionOverlay::paint` takes the composition mode and fills a page's runs as one path, so two overlapping runs tint the page once. The loupe paints the selection the same way. *Regression:* `ui.composition.a selection tints the paper clearly and keeps its glyphs at full contrast on either page colour` (`[selectionink][I-15]`), over `columns-two.pdf` on a plain and an inverted page, graded by WCAG 2's contrast ratio: the selected paper at least **1.6:1** from the clean paper, the glyphs at least **7:1** from the paper under them, and the band blue. **Measured after the fix:** plain **1.82:1** and **9.81:1**; inverted **2.15:1** and **7.94:1** -- the inverted blue was first `#1F4E86`, which gave the glyphs 6.93:1, and was darkened rather than the threshold lowered. **Planted controls, two, each fired:** the old colour and blend (plain 1.12:1 and 5.10:1, inverted glyphs 5.74:1, neither band blue); a multiply on the inverted page too (1.00:1 -- the selection invisible there). *What it does not cover:* how the tint reads on a real monitor and to a person -- **judged by the user on the rebuilt binary the same day: *"It fixed."*** -- and the paper of a document that is not white, which none of this corpus has.

**I-14 · A double click selected nothing and a triple click a word.** *Mechanism:* `ui/viewer/PageView` counts a gesture's clicks in `mousePressEvent` (D5-8: two a word, three a line, four a block), and its comment said a double click *"also arrives as a press through `QWidget::mouseDoubleClickEvent`'s default"*. But `PageView` is a `QAbstractScrollArea`, whose own `mouseDoubleClickEvent` IGNORES the event -- and Qt delivers the second press of each pair to a widget as a `MouseButtonDblClick` INSTEAD of a press, the third as a press, the fourth as a double click again. So every second click was lost: two clicks counted 1 (a caret), three counted 2 (a word), four still 2. *Found* by **M-01's first walk** (2026-10-02), step 2: *"I can only do a triple-click to select the words, double-click and quadruple-click do nothing."* *Why the suite missed it:* A-43 (`ui.` and its `ui.composition.` twin, `[A-43]`) clicked by sending `MouseButtonPress` repeatedly -- a sequence no platform delivers -- and `tst_selecttool.cpp`'s header named the real pointer as M-01's without naming the event sequence as a back door (convention 19). *Fix:* `PageView::mouseDoubleClickEvent` hands the event to `mousePressEvent`, whose counter recognises it by time and place like any press; the stale comment corrected in place. It reaches every tool, as a press always did: a fast second click on the Hand, Marquee Zoom, Loupe or Snapshot tool is now a press rather than a release with none before it. *Regression:* `ui.a double click event is the next click of the gesture and not a lost one` (`[selecttool][I-14]`), Qt's sequence event by event, the count and the selection read after each; and both A-43 cases' click helpers now send that sequence. **Planted control:** the override calling `QAbstractScrollArea`'s handler again -- the I-14 case and A-43 failed (counts 1, 2, 2 against 2, 3, 4), as on the walk. *What it does not cover:* whether the platform pairs the clicks that way is Qt's, seen only by a real pointer -- M-01's re-walk.

**I-13 · Freeing a dropped document's text stalled the UI thread 36–51 ms.** *Mechanism:* `core/text/DocumentIndex` freed what it let go of where it let go of it. When the last handle of a document detaches, `dropEntry` erases the entry, and with it every page's model, reading and searchable forms -- for a 900-page document, hundreds of thousands of small allocations, freed on the UI thread, where every `detach` runs: `FolderSearch::closeCurrent` after each file, `DocumentWindow`'s teardown when a tab closes, `ComparisonView` when a comparison closes. `evict` (the budget) and `invalidate` (a command's scope) freed pages the same way. *Found* by T14's bench (A-112), which recorded the UI thread's longest stall per column and showed **45–48 ms twice in every folder search** against 11–12 ms elsewhere, left ungraded because A2's quantity is tile latency; *measured* by a throwaway probe timing every UI-thread step of `FolderSearch` over 5 ms: **`index_.detach` 36.1–51.4 ms, once for `pages-900.pdf` and once for `text-900.pdf`, nothing else over 5 ms**; a probe in `dropEntry` then measured **42.0 ms freeing 900 pages when the find column's searched tab closed**. *Why the suite missed it:* the fake documents are a few pages, and freeing them takes microseconds; no case timed the UI thread. *Fix:* the index hands what it lets go of -- as shared pointers -- to a releaser of its own, one thread started the first time it is needed, which frees it there; the UI thread moves pointers. `dropEntry`, `evict` and `invalidate` all release through it, a page still held elsewhere (a selection's reading) lives until its last holder lets go, and the releaser drains before the index's entries are destroyed. *Regression:* `unit.a dropped evicted or invalidated document's pages are freed off the calling thread` (`[index][I-13]`): with the releaser held, a page the index held alone is still ALIVE after `detach`, after the budget's eviction and after a whole-document `invalidate` -- so the calling thread did not free it -- and gone once the releaser runs, while a page a selection holds keeps its reading. **Planted controls, four, each fired:** each of the three paths freeing in place again, and a releaser that never lets go. **After it**, `bench_surface --stage5` twice: **the UI thread's longest stall during a folder search 10.8–11.8 ms, 0 stalls over the frame budget in any column**, the viewport figures unchanged (folder search 7.80–8.10 ms against the viewer alone 7.73–8.28); `bench_pipeline --stage5`: the folder column 13.96 ms, 0 inversions. `ctest` **1393/1393 on `x64-release` and on `x64-debug`**, the same four named skips; `gates` 133/133; `checks` clean. *What it does not cover:* the time the UI thread now spends is measured by the bench over the real files, not by the case; the releaser's own thread is one more thread of the process, and the freeing still costs the machine the same CPU, on another core.

**I-12 · `bench_pipeline --stage5` counted priority inversions that were not.** *Mechanism:* A-29's count was inferred, not observed: a background job whose START STAMP fell between a viewport tile's `enqueue()` returning and that tile's own start stamp. The stamps are taken in the bench's timing wrappers, around the adapter call -- after the worker has taken the job off the queue, released the lock and built its result objects. So a job picked with no tile queued, then overtaken in that interval by a tile's `enqueue()`, was stamped inside the tile's wait. *Found* by T14's re-reading, which counted 2 a run where T3 had counted 0, and *proved* by a throwaway probe recording each job's pick ordinal (the scheduler's `started` count as the worker reached the wrapper, exact with one worker) and the count before each tile's enqueue: over four runs every counted inversion -- 1, 0, 2 and 0 of them -- was the same event, **the column's first job, `text:<doc>/0`, pick ordinal 1, picked before the first tile `2/0/0,0` was queued**, its stamp 0.2-0.5 µs after the tile's enqueue returned. **I-09 · A gate case that named several diagnostics asserted only the last.** *Mechanism:* `rawpage_add_gate_test` (`tests/gates/CMakeLists.txt`) declared `EXPECT_MATCH` and `EXPECT_NOT_MATCH` as ONE-VALUE keywords to `cmake_parse_arguments`, which keeps only the last of a repeated keyword -- so a case that wrote two or three of them asserted one, and nothing said so. **I-07 · `LayoutAnalyzer` read a column longer than its neighbour in two pieces.** *Mechanism:* the reading order is recursive XY-cut, horizontal cuts first (D5-6). On a page whose right column ends above the left column's last paragraphs, every paragraph break of the left column BELOW the right column's end is a full-width gap no text crosses -- so the page was cut into a two-column band and the left column's tail, and read: the left column's top, the right column, then the rest of the left. The generator's order -- and a reader's -- is the left column whole, then the right. *Why the suite missed it:* A-38's labelled pages either span a headline or footnote across both columns or end their columns level, so no labelled page had a short column above a paragraph break of the long one; and nothing compared two layouts of one text until T12. *Found* by T12's comparison of `report-v1.pdf` with `report-v2.pdf`: one sentence added to page 2's first paragraph re-flowed six words from the end of the left column to the top of the right, and they were reported deleted from the end of the page and inserted in its middle; on page 1 the moved paragraph came out as some thirty small replacements. *Fix:* a band that splits into columns takes the bands directly below it whose blocks overlap ONE of its columns and no other, while the gap stays a paragraph's (at most two cut thresholds), and each column is then read whole; a spanning block, or a line set further apart (a footer), still ends the columns. *Regression:* `unit.layout reads a column longer than its neighbour whole before the next column`, with its controls -- a spanning line and a footer far below are still read after both columns -- and A-95 over the report pair. **A-38 re-run after the fix:** see A-38. **Planted control:** the continuation removed -- the unit case and A-95 both failed. *What it does not cover:* a column continued across a gap wider than two cut thresholds, which is read as a band of its own after the columns -- the rule that keeps a footer last.

**I-06 · Reflow view lost the reader's place on a page shown turned.** *Mechanism:* D5-16's place is a block, read from the page view's top edge on the way in and put back at it on the way out, and T11 said both in page-space HEIGHTS: the way in handed `blockAtTop` the page-space y under the middle of the edge, and the way back (`PageView::putPageHeightAtTop`) converted one point -- the crop box's left edge at the block's top -- and kept its device y.
