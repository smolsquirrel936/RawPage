# `golden/` — reference rasters

**231 PNGs, 65 corpus files, 77 pages, three resolutions, one rasterizer.** Produced by
`tools/golden-update` — 198 at Stage 2 T17, 24 more at Stage 3 T1 for the six corpus files that
task added, and 9 more at Stage 4 T1 for its three — each one filtered capture that regenerated
none of the baselines before it — and compared on every `ctest` run by `tests/golden`
(`render.golden.*`). This directory is **data, not code** — nothing links it, and
`Tech-Stack-Plan.md` §4.1 lists it beside `i18n/`, `res/` and `corpus/`.

## What a green golden run claims — and what it does not

> **The product rasterizes our own corpus the same way it did when these baselines were captured,
> on this rasterizer, at these three resolutions.**

It does **not** claim the output is *correct*. A baseline captured from a wrong renderer is green
forever, which is why manual case **M-03** (`Stage2-Rendering-Testing-Guide.md` §6) puts a person
beside Acrobat, SumatraPDF and Firefox. And it claims nothing about **real-world PDFs**: every file
under `corpus/` is generated by us (decision **D2-11**, `Stage2-Rendering-Plan.md` §3), and a
generator emits only what its author already understood. `corpus/PROVENANCE.md` carries that bound
and three narrower ones — the JPEG 2000 stream is one coding pass, the JPEGs are baseline 4:4:4, and
the embedded fonts are sixteen rectilinear glyphs.

The sign-off has to say *"consistent with the reference over the generated corpus"* rather than
*"rendering is correct"*. The second is a claim this evidence cannot support.

## The two files, and why they are two

| File | Who writes it | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `manifest.json` | **a person** | The **declaration**: which files, which pages, which resolutions, which are machine-dependent, which are excluded and why, and the comparison thresholds. Reviewed like code. `golden-update` never writes it. |
| `captured.json` | `golden-update` | The **measurement**: the PDFium version, the capture timestamp, the `--reason`, and the fonts each page actually resolved. Do not hand-edit. The timestamp is **UTC**, which is what the field is called — the first capture reads `2026-09-05T16:34Z` on a task dated 2026-09-06, because this machine is eight hours ahead of it. A local timestamp in a committed record is worse than an offset that has to be explained once. |

That split is `cmake/PdfiumAssert.cmake`'s, deliberately: **expected values live in the file a
person edits, found values live where the machine put them, and the asymmetry is the check.**
`UPDATES.md` is the append-only record of every regeneration and the reason given for it.

## Regenerating

```powershell
. .\scripts\env.ps1
.\build\x64-release\bin\golden-update.exe --explain          # thresholds, census, where things live
.\build\x64-release\bin\golden-update.exe --check-manifest    # every corpus file is accounted for
.\build\x64-release\bin\golden-update.exe --survey            # measure corpus/, print a skeleton
.\build\x64-release\bin\golden-update.exe --reason "why the baselines moved"
```

**`--reason` is mandatory and the refusal is the point** (decision **D2-3**). A golden baseline is a
claim about *one rasterizer*, and the cheapest way to make a rendering regression disappear is to
re-capture the baseline from the code that broke it — after which the suite is green, the diff shows
nothing but binary files, and nobody learns what moved. `Implementation-Plan.md` §2 says baselines
are regenerated *"only through `tools/golden-update` with a stated reason"*; this is the enforcement
of that sentence, and guide case **A-50** drives the executable to prove the refusal happens.

A failing comparison writes `<case>-expected.png`, `<case>-actual.png` and `<case>-diff.png` into
`<build>/golden-failures/`, never into this directory — a diff written beside the references would
leave the next `git status` full of files that look like new baselines.

## What is baselined, and what is not

**Page 0 of every corpus file the engine will open, at 96 / 144 / 192 DPI.** A further page is
captured only where the *pixels* differ for a reason page 0 cannot show: the four rotations of
`rotated-pages`, the three ways `rotate-inherited`'s pages reach their rotation, the four media boxes of `mixed-page-sizes`, the three revisions of
`incremental-3gen`, the second annotation page of `annots-mixed`, and the **last** page of
`pages-900` — which is what proves a 30 × 30 page tree is walked to its end rather than to its first
leaf. `cover-odd-count`, `rtl-binding` and `outline-labels` are *layout* fixtures; their later pages
are ordinary ink and `PagePresentation`'s arithmetic is `tests/unit`'s subject.

**Five corpus files are excluded, each with a stated reason in the manifest**, and every one of them
is a file the engine will not hand us a page for — four it refuses outright and one that opens with
zero pages. Refusing is correct behaviour and is asserted by guide cases A-42 and A-43; a golden
entry here would be a second copy of that claim with no pixels in it. **Every corpus file must be a
case or an excluded row**, checked both by `golden-update --check-manifest` and by the suite, which
is `corpus/make-corpus.py --check-provenance`'s rule in a second subject.

## The three things that make a baseline reproducible

1. **`RenderFlags::NoNativeText` is in the golden flag set.** `core/render/RenderRequest.h` has
   carried that flag since T9 with the comment *"required for reproducible goldens"*, and this is
   what cashes it: without it PDFium may hand text to the OS renderer, and a baseline becomes a
   claim about this machine's ClearType and gamma settings. The set is
   `Annotations | NoNativeText` — annotations because that is what `ui/viewer/PageView` draws by
   default, so a baseline is about the product rather than a subset of it.

2. **The base-14 faces come from PDFium's own metric-compatible copies**, because
   `pdf/render/FontResolverBridge` declines them by name. That is issue **I-13**, found at T16 by a
   control case *one task before these baselines existed*. Had it not been found, every image here
   would have been a claim about whichever Segoe UI this Windows shipped.

3. **A substituted face is not reproducible, and two cases say so.** `machineDependent` marks them;
   their capture-time substitutions are recorded in `captured.json`; and the suite **skips such a
   case by name** on a machine that resolves differently rather than reporting a rendering
   regression (guide case A-62's rule — *report not applicable explicitly rather than pass
   vacuously* — applied to a second subject).

   - `ordinary/font-not-embedded.pdf` names two families installed on no machine. Expected.
   - `malformed/bad-name-hex-escape.pdf` **was found by the capture, not predicted.** Its broken
     `#`-escape truncates `/Helvetica` to `/Helve`, which is not a standard-14 name, so the bridge
     matches a real installed face (Segoe UI here) instead of declining. The damage the file is
     named for is what makes its baseline a claim about this machine's fonts.

   A case asserts that **no other** baseline resolved a face off this disk — which is I-13's
   tripwire: if the base-14 decline is ever removed, every image moves at once and that case names
   the cause in one line instead of leaving it to look like a renderer regression.

## Storage

PNG, through QtGui's built-in handler — a raster class that needs no display and no
`QGuiApplication`, and a module already in this project's Qt link closure through `rawpage_ui`, so
`tools/license-audit` sees nothing new.

**Baselines whose every pixel has `R == G == B` are stored as 8-bit grey** — 171 of the 198 at T17,
192 of 222 re-counted at Stage 3 T1 from each PNG's colour-type byte, and **198 of 231** re-counted
the same way at Stage 4 T1, whose nine are six grey and three RGB — `cropbox-margins.pdf` is the
one of its three files that carries colour — and expanded back to RGB
on read, which is lossless in both directions and took the T17 tree from 30.5 MB to 13.1 MB. The comparison reads the three colour bytes on both sides, so the encoding is
invisible above `readPng`.

**The encoder is not pinned, and that is accepted.** A Qt upgrade could re-encode identical pixels
into different bytes and show as a `git` diff. It cannot turn a green suite red, because **the
comparison is over decoded pixels and never over file bytes** — which is `corpus/`'s
byte-identical rule deliberately *not* applied here: a corpus file's bytes are the fixture, a
baseline's pixels are.

## The threshold

Perceptual with a stated threshold (**D2-13**), and it is **two** numbers rather than one:

| | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `channelTolerance` 8 · `differingFraction` 0.1 % | a pixel *differs* above a per-channel delta of 8, and at most a thousandth of a page may |
| `grossChannelTolerance` 96 · `grossFraction` 0.01 % | a pixel is *grossly* wrong above 96, and almost none may be |

One number cannot separate the two failures it has to tell apart. Antialiasing drift is **many**
pixels each off by a **little**; a real regression — a shifted glyph, a missing hairline, a wrong
colour — is either many pixels or **a few off by a lot**. A single threshold loose enough to absorb
the first accepts the second.

These are not aspirational figures. On the machine that captured them a re-render of the unchanged
corpus differs from its baseline in **zero pixels at all three resolutions**, so the whole of both
budgets is headroom for a future PDFium's rounding.
